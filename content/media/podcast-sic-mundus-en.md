In the first episode of Sic Mundus, we discussed how large language models work and how they are being used in religious life.

## Language models and religious practice

Many contemporary language models are trained to predict tokens. Their behaviour depends on training data, model architecture, subsequent training and the context in which they are used. We discussed the difficulty of interpreting model behaviour and aligning it with human intentions.

We distinguished several types of digital religious tools: prayer applications, specialised chatbots drawing on religious texts, simulations of religious figures, robots used in ritual settings, and general-purpose language models. Examples included Magisterium AI, Mindar and ChatGPT.

## Religious authority

We discussed possible reasons users may trust AI with spiritual questions: anthropomorphism, agreeable responses, privacy and the accessibility of advice outside religious institutions. Whether these tools contribute to religious individualisation or change the authority of existing institutions remains an empirical question.

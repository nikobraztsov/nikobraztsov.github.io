On 25 November, I gave a public lecture on agent-based modelling (ABM) at the European University at St. Petersburg, organised by the School of Computational Social Sciences.

I used four studies to discuss how sociologists develop and test explanations of social phenomena through computer simulations. The examples concerned opinion polarisation through argument exchange among like-minded individuals, changes in European marriage patterns associated with shifts in educational attainment, the role of network structure in epidemic transmission, and the debate over whether greater social connectivity leads to polarisation.

I also introduced my current research on the characteristics of social ties that help explain the persistence of minority practices under majority influence.
